# Liyu POS — Go-to-Market Plan

**Launch posture:** founder-led, local, evidence-first. Start only after the critical sale/payment/inventory path has passed staging and real-device checks. Until then, recruit discovery partners and run scripted demos with sample data—not live-money pilots.

## 1. First customer segment

Target owner-operated **cafes, bars/tej houses and casual restaurants with one outlet** in Addis Ababa. Prioritize venues with bottled-drink stock, several staff roles, table service or shift handovers, and an owner who reviews cash and stock daily. This segment has a visible daily workflow that matches the product: receive stock → issue/accept custody → take order → Kitchen/Bar ticket → collect payment → close shift.

Do not start with national chains, formal hotel groups or government buyers. Their procurement, fiscal, integration and uptime requirements can overwhelm an unproven support operation.

## 2. Find the first 10, 100 and 1,000 customers

### First 10: learn and serve manually

1. Build a list of 50 reachable venues in two adjacent Addis neighborhoods from field observation and referrals; record buyer, outlet type, current till/ticket/inventory method and contact permission.
2. Conduct 15–20 short owner interviews before selling. Ask for a recent real example of a missed ticket, stock discrepancy or closeout delay; ask what they tried and what it costs them.
3. Demonstrate the exact workflow on a phone/tablet with clearly labeled sample data. Let the owner and one cashier try it.
4. Recruit 3–5 design partners only after staging tests; agree a written pilot boundary, support hours, data handling, backup, rollback and success measures.
5. Convert to paid plans through a clear introductory offer. Do not exchange a discount for a fabricated review or quote.

### First 100: repeat the local playbook

- Focus on one segment and repeatable onboarding, not broad feature expansion.
- Add an onboarding checklist, product import template, role-specific 10-minute training and weekly first-month check-in.
- Ask satisfied active users for an introduction; reward a referral with a transparent service credit after the referred outlet becomes paid.
- Partner prospectively with bookkeepers/accountants, printer/POS hardware sellers and hospitality consultants. Sign no partnership claim until an agreement exists.
- Track demos, conversion, churn, onboarding hours, support per outlet and weekly active outlets by cohort.
- Expand neighborhood-by-neighborhood or through a trained local implementation partner only when service quality holds.

### First 1,000: productize distribution

- Establish repeatable city launch kits: setup, training, local device/printer checks, support and payment collection.
- Hire and certify implementation/support staff; measure first-week activation and customer health.
- Use referral partners and practical case studies with written customer consent and verifiable figures.
- Build self-serve billing and server-enforced plan limits before relying on online conversion.
- Expand across Ethiopia in clusters. Enter another country only after validating language, currency, payments, tax/fiscal requirements, hosting, support and partner economics.
- Do not assume organic virality or paid-ad scale; prove channel CAC and retention first.

## 3. Launch strategy

### Pre-launch gates

- Staging migration history and RLS confirmed.
- One complete sale/payment/FIFO/inventory/receipt lifecycle and retry checked directly in database.
- Android and desktop online → offline → local sale → restart → reconnect → one server sale behavior recorded.
- Kitchen/Bar ticket routing and state transitions demonstrated across two sessions.
- Credit disabled or cashier collection workflow fully verified.
- Support terms, backup/restore process, data export/exit path, pricing and plan entitlements documented.
- Landing page mock numbers and unsupported claims removed or plainly labeled as illustrative demo.

### Launch offer

Run a four-week invitation-only pilot with one outlet per owner and a capped number of businesses. Offer hands-on setup and a discounted first two months in exchange for weekly feedback and permission to observe—not for a guaranteed testimonial. Make the customer’s data export and support contact clear.

## 4. Brand positioning and messaging

**Positioning:** “Daily sales, shift and stock control for Ethiopian cafes, restaurants and bars.”

**Messages to test:**

- “Know what sold and how each shift closed.”
- “Send one saved order to the configured Kitchen or Bar queue.”
- “Record stock received and the staff member who accepted custody.”
- “Designed for local workflows and being tested for interrupted connections.”

Avoid “100% offline,” “theft detection,” “instant exact profit,” “provider verified Telebirr/CBE,” “MOR certified,” “zero training,” and outcome percentages until proved. The public landing page contains sample transactions and strong discrepancy/profit language; clarify that sample data is illustrative and remove statements unsupported by measured deployments.

## 5. Landing-page recommendations

1. Put one concrete buyer value in the headline and identify cafes/bars/restaurants, not generic “all businesses.”
2. Show real product screenshots/video using a demo tenant; label data as sample.
3. Demonstrate the end-to-end flow: cashier sale → order/ticket → shift close → inventory source record.
4. State payment semantics accurately: local tender recording is not wallet-provider confirmation.
5. Explain device/browser requirements and tested offline boundaries; distinguish “works from loaded session” from cold offline start.
6. Show pricing in ETB, what is included, branch/staff limits, support response, trial duration and cancellation.
7. Include a “book a 15-minute demo” option and a WhatsApp contact only if staffed and consent-compliant.
8. Replace static “live” dashboard/mock transaction values with clearly labeled demonstration data.
9. Do not publish unsupported profitability or theft-detection claims.
10. Add FAQ about data ownership, exports, backups, migration, devices and what happens when internet returns.

## 6. Content marketing and SEO

Create useful English and Amharic pages around concrete needs: “restaurant shift cash reconciliation,” “bar stock handover,” “cafe stock count,” “kitchen order ticket display,” and “POS that queues work during internet interruptions.” Keep country/city terms only when the service is actually available there. Use short screen-recorded demos and explain workflows; no keyword stuffing or scraped competitor copy.

Measure qualified demo requests rather than page views alone. Provide a WhatsApp/phone CTA with explicit consent and response expectations; avoid uploading customer contacts or staff lists to ad platforms.

## 7. Partnerships and referrals

Prospect, do not claim existing arrangements, with:

- Local accountants/bookkeepers who reconcile daily sales and expenses.
- Receipt-printer, cash-drawer, tablet and network-equipment sellers/repair shops.
- Hospitality training and restaurant-consulting providers.
- Local payment providers only for a future verified integration.
- Business associations and restaurant owner communities for educational demos.

Use a written referral policy: disclose any commission, protect customer data, and pay only after a retained paid customer—not for a lead alone.

## 8. Paid advertising experiments

Avoid large paid campaigns before pricing and retention evidence. Test small, capped campaigns after the landing page and pilot are credible:

| Test | Audience/creative | Budget rule | Success event |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Addis cafe/bar owners; short real KOT/shift-close demo | Cap at ETB 3,000–5,000 for 7 days | Qualified owner demo booked, not clicks |
| B | Amharic vs English tutorial creative | Same spend and target geography | Cost per held demo and lead quality |
| C | Stock custody explainer vs cash-close explainer | Small separate cohorts | Paid pilot conversion and time-to-close |

Stop a test if it yields clicks but no qualified demos, or if one converted customer’s gross profit cannot plausibly repay the campaign cost. The budgets are proposed experiment caps, not market rates.

## 9. Practical 30-day launch campaign

| Days | Activity | Deliverable / measurement |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Finish technical release gates and prepare demo data/device | Signed go/no-go checklist, clean demo tenant, data/privacy sheet |
| 4–7 | Interview 10 owners and 5 staff; observe real close where invited | Problem notes, current workflow map, top 3 purchase objections |
| 8–10 | Revise onboarding/demo based on interviews; present pricing options | Demo script, clear offer, objections log |
| 11–14 | Run 5 owner demos; invite suitable businesses into pilot | Demos held, activation candidates, no unsupported promises |
| 15–21 | Onboard first pilots; observe first sales/shift close; daily support review | First-week activation, incidents, onboarding/support hours |
| 22–26 | Review stock, order and shift workflows with users; fix only high-impact defects | Confirmed workflow gaps and regression checks |
| 27–30 | Ask for paid conversion and referral; publish a real feature demo with consent | Paid conversion, referral leads, renewal objections, next month gate |

If technical gates are incomplete, use days 1–30 for discovery and prototype demonstrations only; do not process live customer money or rely on a live inventory ledger.

| Day | Action |

| Tuesday | Owner visits/interviews; record objections and current workflow with permission. |

| Sunday | Prepare one educational post and review privacy/claim accuracy before scheduling. |

## 11. Metrics

- Prospects contacted → qualified → demo booked → demo held → pilot started → paid.
- Demo-to-paid conversion and median days from first contact to first live sale.
- Time to first product, first sale, first completed shift, and first successful sync.
- Active outlets per week; share with a completed shift close; 30/60/90-day retention.
- Onboarding hours, support contacts, failure/retry count and incidents per outlet.
- Customer-reported confidence in cash close and stock count, paired with actual records where consented.
- CAC by channel, ARPA, monthly churn, gross margin and referral rate.
- Offline recovery success and duplicate sale/payment/inventory counts on test devices.

## 12. Go/no-go rules

Do not scale paid acquisition if a customer cannot confidently answer “was this sale committed?” or if database/inventory mismatch remains. Do not expand beyond the initial segment until most new outlets can complete setup with a repeatable checklist and support load is economically compatible with the subscription price.
