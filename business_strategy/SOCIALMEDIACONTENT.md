# Liyu POS — Social Media Content Pack

## Publishing guardrails

- Use real product screens only when available; otherwise label the video **“Sample data / product demo.”**
- Do not show invented customer names, sales results, testimonials, business logos or “live” revenue as customer evidence.
- Do not promise “theft detection,” exact profit, fiscal certification, guaranteed offline continuity or provider-confirmed wallet payments.
- Say **“record Telebirr/CBE Birr as a tender”**, not “Liyu verifies Telebirr/CBE.”
- Offline checkout and end-to-end sync still require real-device/staging confirmation. Until that gate passes, describe it as “designed for queued work during interruptions; ask for a controlled demo.”
- Obtain consent before filming a venue, employee, customer or business data.

## 1. Ten TikTok / Instagram Reels concepts

1. **From table to ticket:** Add a sample table order and show its configured Kitchen/Bar destination; label it sample data.
2. **One shift, one close:** Explain opening float, cash tender and physical cash count without showing a fake business result.
3. **Stock received vs stock sold:** Animate 100 received, 20 assigned to custody, 2 sold; label as an illustrative example and clarify issue is not a sale.
4. **Role-based day:** Fast cuts: waiter saves order, bartender marks preparation, cashier records payment, owner reviews close.
5. **Manual digital tender:** Show how a cashier records a Telebirr or CBE Birr tender; overlay “Recorded by staff—provider confirmation happens separately.”
6. **What a stock handover means:** Show issuer, recipient, product, quantity and acceptance; explain responsibility changes without a second branch-stock deduction.
7. **Kitchen vs Bar routing:** Change sample category/product station setting and show resulting station ticket after save.
8. **A clearer closeout:** Show where expected cash comes from and where the physical count is entered; no fabricated variance.
9. **Amharic + English interface:** Show one real screen in both locales and invite staff feedback on wording.
10. **A founder’s workflow walk-through:** Record a demo checklist on a phone/tablet and ask local owners which step is hardest today.

## 2. Five short-video scripts

### Script 1 — “Where did the stock go?”

**Hook (0–3s):** “Received, issued, and sold are three different events.”

**Scenes:** Use a neutral whiteboard or clearly labeled Liyu demo data. Show `100 received`; then `20 issued to Bar`; then `2 sold`. Never show these as an actual customer’s ledger.

**Narration:** “Receiving adds stock to the branch balance. Issuing assigns custody to a staff member; it is not a sale. When a sale is recorded, branch stock decreases once. In this example, 100 received stays 100 after a 20-unit handover, then becomes 98 after selling 2. The custody example is 20 accepted and 18 remaining, assuming the sale is allocated to that worker.”

**On-screen text:** `Illustrative example • 100 → issue 20: branch 100 • sell 2: branch 98 • custody: 20 → 18`.

**CTA:** “Ask us to walk through stock receiving and custody with your own process.”

## 4. Five LinkedIn posts

### Post 1 — Building for the operating sequence

Hospitality software is most useful when it follows the actual work: inventory comes in, a waiter takes the order, Kitchen or Bar prepares it, the cashier records payment, and the owner reviews the close.

That is the sequence we are working to make clear in Liyu POS. The current product source includes table orders, configured preparation stations, stock movements, shift reporting and staff roles. We are still validating database deployment and real-device reliability before making production claims.

If you operate a cafe, bar or restaurant in Ethiopia, I’d value a 20-minute conversation about the point where your daily process breaks down.

### Post 2 — Separate the stock events

“Received,” “issued,” and “sold” should not be collapsed into one number.

In the current Liyu model, branch inventory is the stock balance, while a handover is a custody/accountability record. A sale is the event that consumes branch stock. That distinction is important in beverage operations where stock can be assigned to a staff member before customers buy it.

We’re looking for operators who can help us validate this workflow with real shift procedures—not just review a feature list.

### Post 3 — Recorded payment versus confirmed payment

A POS can record “Telebirr” as the tender method without having a live connection to Telebirr.

Liyu currently records supported methods such as cash, Telebirr, CBE Birr and bank transfer in its POS flow. A manually entered method is not provider verification. Clear records and reconciliation matter; the software should not imply a payment settled unless it has actual settlement evidence.

We want this distinction to stay clear for cashiers and owners.

### Post 4 — Local-first is a release criterion

Offline support is not proven by a cached screen. It must preserve a local sale, receipt and pending state, then synchronize once and reconcile against the server after connectivity returns.

Liyu has an IndexedDB outbox and signed catalog price snapshots in source. The remaining work is to verify the full lifecycle on target Android and desktop devices and against a staging database. We will publish test evidence when it is available.

### Post 5 — A narrow first market

We are focusing first on owner-operated cafes, bars/tej houses and small restaurants rather than trying to serve every business type at once.

The strongest product fit appears to be where sales, shift close, stock handover and Kitchen/Bar tickets meet. That is a hypothesis we’re validating through owner interviews and controlled pilots.

If you run one of these businesses in Addis, what is the most painful daily handover you manage?
